# Project Reclaim 100-Person Startup Capital Package

**Status:** Planning proposal; non-binding; not a vendor bid  
**Reference date:** August 12, 2026 (Pacific Time)  
**Prepared by:** Manus AI

## Purpose and Operating Boundary

This package is a capital-startup planning model for a **100-participant Project Reclaim launch** focused on fire-mitigation support, vegetation and material recovery, wood reclamation, hand-tool-based craft and carpentry training, site rehabilitation, and documented reuse. It is designed to make the physical work possible while supporting skilled, paid, voluntary participation.

The model assumes **ten supervised work pods of ten participants**. It does **not** assume that all 100 participants simultaneously operate chain saws, chippers, loaders, shop machinery, or vehicles. Hazardous tasks require documented task qualification, supervision, equipment-specific controls, and a site-specific safety plan. California’s portable-chain-saw rule addresses leg protection, chain brakes, inspection, fueling, operator instruction, and related controls; OSHA’s logging standard also addresses PPE, first aid, communications, equipment, vehicles, training, and active work areas.[5] [6]

> **Decision rule:** Buy the shared platform that creates repeatable capability, but rent, subcontract, or defer specialized equipment until an approved project has a demonstrated work order, qualified operator, authorized site, and total-cost comparison.

## What This Package Deliberately Excludes

This is **not** an all-in cost for a 100-person residential, behavioral-health, recovery, or corrections-reentry program. The following require separately scoped, professionally reviewed budgets and are excluded:

| Excluded category | Why it is separate |
|---|---|
| Land acquisition, leases, environmental due diligence, and title | Site-specific economics and legal risk vary materially by property and jurisdiction |
| Building rehabilitation, housing, kitchens, utility extensions, and resident furnishing | Requires property condition assessment, design, code, permits, contracting, and operating model |
| Payroll, benefits, taxes, workers’ compensation, and ongoing operating reserve | Recurring workforce costs require labor-model and funding-source assumptions |
| Licensed mental-health, substance-use, therapy, and medical services | Must be planned and delivered through qualified/licensed providers and approved agreements |
| Probation, parole, corrections, or justice-agency programs | Requires confirmed agency agreements, consent pathways, data governance, and legal review |
| Permits, timber/vegetation approvals, environmental/cultural review, public-land authorization, and utility work | Site and jurisdiction dependent; no equipment purchase creates authority to perform work |
| Sales/use tax, registration, delivery, finance charges, dealer setup, freight, and site mobilization | Quote-, jurisdiction-, and contract-specific; not reliably included in public benchmarks |

## Procurement Gates Before Any Purchase Order

Every purchase package should pass the following gates. The owner should retain the decision record, at least three comparable quotes where market conditions allow, total cost of ownership, required operator qualifications, insurance and safety checks, and a clear decision to buy, rent, lease, or subcontract.

| Gate | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Work need | Authorized project scope, forecast utilization, and responsible lead |
| Site authority | Property owner, agency, or relevant public-land authorization; no map layer is proof of permission |
| People and training | Assigned supervisor, task qualification, emergency plan, and ratio of qualified operators to participants |
| Safety and maintenance | Manufacturer documentation, inspections, preventive-maintenance plan, parts/service path, and PPE requirements |
| Financial control | Vendor quotes, taxes/fees, freight, insurance, residual value, lease/rental alternative, and 15% physical-program contingency |
| Legal and data | Contracts, certificates, permitting review, insurance/bonding, privacy controls, and data-rights review where applicable |

## Staged Purchase Sequence

The package is intentionally structured so that Project Reclaim does not need to acquire all equipment on day one.

| Stage | Release condition | Priority purchases |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Mobilize | Organization, insurance, safety system, training leads, and first authorized scopes are in place | PPE, field tools, radios, trauma/first-aid, enclosed trailers, basic crew vehicles, hand-tool craft benches, tracking system |
| 2. Field delivery | Recurring authorized work orders and qualified operator coverage | Chippers, compact loader(s), dump trailer(s), water trailers, limited saw kits, site controls |
| 3. Material recovery | Documented material inflow, storage site, handling plan, and buyer/reuse pathway | Sawmill, forklift, racks, moisture/metal tools, drying/storage system |
| 4. Shop production | Approved facility, ventilation/electrical/guarding plan, instructor qualifications, and market/reuse demand | Guarded woodworking equipment and dust collection |
| 5. Scale | Demonstrated utilization, maintenance capacity, and multi-project demand | Additional fleet, loaders, excavator/tractor capacity, and container shop expansion |

## Basis, Time, Assumptions, Sources, and Compliance Disclosure

**Basis.** This is a capital-planning procurement model for a 100-participant, ten-pod operating concept. The $4,341,439.85 calculated total uses quantity × planning-unit allowance across listed capital and opening-supply items, plus a 15% contingency on $3,427,339 of physical/operational inputs. It is not a formal bid, valuation, vendor quote, operating budget, or workforce cost model.

**Time.** Sources and public price observations were checked on **August 12, 2026 (Pacific Time)**. Equipment prices, dealer availability, taxes, freight, and specifications can change.

**Assumptions.** The model assumes supervised, task-qualified work pods; shared equipment rather than 100 simultaneous high-hazard operators; a separate professional pathway for clinical, recovery, and justice partnerships; and a staged purchase approach. It excludes the categories listed above, including land, housing, payroll, clinical services, and project-specific permitting.

**Sources and confidence.** Safety requirements are grounded in California and federal regulator material and are high-confidence for planning controls. Equipment prices are public manufacturer/dealer/listing observations and are medium-confidence planning benchmarks, not California vendor quotes.[1] [2] [3] [4] The full source log is available in `STARTUP_PACKAGE_SOURCES.md`.

**Compliance.** This is research and analysis only, not personalized financial advice. It does not replace procurement counsel, licensed safety/clinical leadership, environmental review, insurance/bonding advice, tax advice, public-land authorization, or agency agreements.
