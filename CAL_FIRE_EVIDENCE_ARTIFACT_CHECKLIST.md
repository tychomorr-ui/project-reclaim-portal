# Project Reclaim

## CAL FIRE Wildfire-Prevention Evidence Artifact Checklist

**Prepared for:** Project Reclaim
**Primary geography:** Mendocino and Lake Counties, California
**Use:** Preparation for a future CAL FIRE Wildfire Prevention Grant solicitation
**Current program status:** The FY 2025–26 Proposition 4 solicitation is closed. This checklist prepares a future application and a partner-led work package; it does not represent that funding is currently open.

## 1. What CAL FIRE application we are preparing

Project Reclaim should not apply for the entire residential, workforce, housing, treatment/referral, equipment, and ecosystem vision as one grant request. The application should be built around one defined wildfire-prevention work package with:

- one eligible lead applicant;
- one documented project geography;
- one authorized scope of work;
- one map and treatment area;
- one budget tied to eligible prevention outputs;
- one responsible safety and supervision structure; and
- one evidence plan that proves what was completed.

The strongest possible future package is likely to be a mapped hazardous-fuels reduction, qualifying defensible-space, evacuation/access, wildfire-prevention planning, or wildfire-prevention education project. The exact category must be confirmed against the next CAL FIRE solicitation.

## 2. The non-negotiable applicant file

Create a folder named `01-Applicant-and-Authority` containing the following:

### 2.1 Eligible-applicant proof — REQUIRED

Collect one of the following, depending on the lead:

- Articles of incorporation and Secretary of State documentation for a qualified nonprofit;
- IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter, if already approved;
- evidence of a pending 501(c)(3) application, if the future guidelines permit application before approval, followed by approval before grant execution;
- Tribal authorization and documentation for the Tribal applicant;
- city, county, special district, resource-conservation district, fire-protection district, community-services district, water district, or other eligible public-entity documentation; or
- joint-powers-authority agreement showing that the participating entities are eligible applicants.

**Open gap:** Project Reclaim’s current eligible-applicant status must be confirmed. If Project Reclaim is not itself eligible, identify the actual lead applicant. Do not describe an informal partner as a pass-through applicant; the FY 2025–26 guidelines prohibited sub-awarding.

### 2.2 Authority to submit — REQUIRED

Prepare:

- board resolution or equivalent authorization to apply and sign;
- signer’s name, title, and authority;
- governing-board approval record, if required by the applicant;
- California Grants Portal account ownership and authorized-user record; and
- a one-page applicant responsibility statement identifying who owns the grant, receives funds, manages procurement, and submits reports.

### 2.3 Payee and registrations — REQUIRED at application or award stage as specified

Prepare the current version of:

- California State Payee Data Record, Standard 204;
- legal name, address, tax identification, and payment information;
- SAM/UEI or other registration only if required by the next state or partner funding structure;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure or organizational policy; and
- insurance and workers’ compensation information for the applicant and contractors.

## 3. The site-control and authorization file

Create `02-Site-Control-and-Authorization`.

### 3.1 Site-control proof — REQUIRED

For every parcel or treatment area, collect the document that proves the applicant can lawfully propose and perform the work:

- deed or ownership record;
- executed lease or license;
- landowner permission letter;
- right-of-entry agreement;
- public-agency work authorization;
- Tribal authorization where applicable;
- easement or access agreement; and
- parcel list with APN, address or legal description, owner, authorized contact, and access limitations.

A real-estate listing, map pin, verbal promise, broker conversation, or property lead is not site control.

### 3.2 Landowner and affected-party permissions — REQUIRED where applicable

Prepare signed letters or agreements that state:

- the exact property or treatment area;
- the authorized work;
- the date range;
- who may enter the land;
- who owns removed material;
- who is responsible for gates, roads, utilities, pets, livestock, and restoration;
- whether photographs, maps, and before/after evidence may be released publicly; and
- who must be contacted if work stops or conditions change.

### 3.3 Site-risk and authorization register — REQUIRED internally

Create a spreadsheet with one row per parcel and these fields:

- project parcel ID;
- APN or legal description;
- owner/authorizing entity;
- access authorization status;
- fire-threat context and source;
- treatment type;
- estimated area or linear measurement;
- adjacent structures/infrastructure;
- environmental or cultural review flag;
- utilities and access hazards;
- insurance requirement;
- unresolved blocker;
- reviewer;
- review date; and
- status: `Planning`, `Proposed`, `Authorization Pending`, `Active`, `Evidence Pending`, `Verified`, or `Rejected`.

## 4. The wildfire-prevention scope file

Create `03-Scope-of-Work`.

### 4.1 Two-page project narrative — REQUIRED

The narrative should answer:

1. What wildfire risk is being addressed?
2. Where is the work located?
3. Who is the eligible applicant?
4. What exact treatment will occur?
5. Why is the treatment eligible under the next CAL FIRE guidelines?
6. Who owns or authorizes the land?
7. Who will perform and supervise the work?
8. What will be measured?
9. What will be completed during the grant period?
10. What is outside the grant scope?

Use direct language such as:

> Project Reclaim proposes a partner-led, mapped wildfire-prevention work package in [verified location]. The work will reduce [verified fuel/access/defensible-space] risk through [specific treatment]. The eligible lead applicant is [verified entity]. Project Reclaim’s role will be [verified role], under written authorization, qualified supervision, documented safety controls, and the release rules described in the application.

Do not call Project Reclaim a fire agency, hotshot crew, emergency-response agency, or licensed contractor unless those facts are independently true and documented.

### 4.2 Treatment specification — REQUIRED

Prepare a technical scope that states:

- treatment type;
- start and end points;
- acreage, linear feet, parcel count, or other measurable unit;
- fuel or vegetation condition being addressed;
- treatment method;
- hand work versus mechanized work;
- material handling and disposal/reuse method;
- access and staging plan;
- seasonal and weather limitations;
- stop-work conditions;
- restoration requirements; and
- acceptance criteria for completion.

### 4.3 Baseline risk evidence — REQUIRED

Collect source-backed evidence such as:

- CAL FIRE or official fire-threat mapping;
- county or Tribal hazard-mitigation plan references;
- Community Wildfire Protection Plan references;
- evacuation-route or access documentation;
- defensible-space or fuel-condition assessment by a qualified reviewer;
- dated field photographs with location and permission metadata;
- fire-history or vegetation-condition data from an authoritative source; and
- documented public-health, public-safety, critical-infrastructure, or community-access rationale.

A general statement that Mendocino or Lake County has high fire risk is not enough. The application needs a documented connection between the selected work area and the stated prevention objective.

## 5. The mapping file

Create `04-Maps-and-Geospatial`.

### 5.1 CAL FIRE portal mapping — REQUIRED

For a hazardous-fuels-reduction project, prepare:

- one project geopoint; and
- treatment-area polygon or polygons for every proposed treatment area.

CAL FIRE’s FY 2025–26 guidance stated that hazardous-fuels applications without treatment-area polygons would not be accepted. A future solicitation may change the technical instructions, so re-check the current mapping guide before submission.

### 5.2 Printable project map — REQUIRED

Create an 8.5-by-11-inch PDF map with:

- project name;
- project tracking number once assigned;
- project proponent;
- north arrow;
- scale bar;
- legend;
- parcel boundaries where legally sourced and licensed;
- treatment boundaries;
- roads and access routes;
- evacuation or critical-access features if relevant;
- nearby structures or infrastructure only where appropriate and privacy-safe;
- source, date, and map-accuracy note; and
- a clear label stating that the map represents proposed treatment, not completed work.

### 5.3 GIS source package — REQUIRED internally

Retain the source files used to create the map:

- shapefile or other format required by the solicitation;
- geopoint and polygon coordinates;
- coordinate reference system;
- source layers and licenses;
- map-production date;
- editor/reviewer; and
- checksum or version identifier.

Do not use proprietary property-line or land-ownership layers unless the project has the right to use them and the source is recorded. A map overlay is not proof of ownership, access, or permission.

## 6. The partner and commitment file

Create `05-Partner-Commitments`.

### 6.1 Lead-applicant commitment — REQUIRED

Obtain a signed letter or resolution that identifies:

- the lead applicant;
- Project Reclaim’s exact role;
- the site and work package;
- who owns the grant relationship;
- who supervises work;
- who manages funds and procurement;
- who approves public communications; and
- what remains subject to later approval.

### 6.2 Landowner or site-authority letter — REQUIRED

This is separate from a general letter of support. It must authorize the actual work area and access.

### 6.3 Fire, county, Tribal, or resource partner letter — STRONGLY RECOMMENDED

Request a letter from the relevant agency, Tribe, fire partner, resource-conservation district, county department, Fire Safe Council, or other qualified organization that confirms only what it is actually willing to confirm:

- the prevention problem;
- the proposed scope;
- the partner’s technical or coordination role;
- any required qualification or review;
- whether the partner is contributing cash, staff, equipment, data, or review; and
- the status of the relationship: `Conversation`, `Letter of Support`, `Letter of Commitment`, `MOU`, or `Executed Agreement`.

Do not call a meeting, email exchange, or general interest a partnership.

### 6.4 Matching-funds or in-kind commitment letters — REQUIRED if claimed

If the budget includes matching funds or services, obtain a signed letter for each contribution stating:

- contributor;
- cash amount or in-kind valuation;
- dates available;
- specific service, equipment, labor, or materials;
- valuation method;
- restrictions; and
- authorized signer.

If a contribution is not documented, remove it from the matching budget rather than describing it as committed.

## 7. The safety, supervision, and qualifications file

Create `06-Safety-and-Qualifications`.

### 7.1 Safety plan — REQUIRED for a credible application

Prepare a project-specific safety plan containing:

- responsible safety lead;
- chain of command;
- daily briefing process;
- hazard assessment;
- PPE requirements;
- heat, smoke, weather, and air-quality controls;
- communications plan;
- first-aid and emergency procedure;
- vehicle and equipment controls;
- public and traffic protection;
- wildlife, utilities, steep-slope, and falling-object controls;
- stop-work authority;
- incident and near-miss reporting; and
- worker and participant grievance route.

### 7.2 Supervisor and contractor qualifications — REQUIRED where applicable

Retain:

- resumes or qualification summaries;
- relevant licenses or certifications;
- chainsaw or equipment qualifications where required;
- first-aid/CPR or other required training;
- contractor license and insurance information;
- workers’ compensation proof;
- vehicle/equipment operator qualifications; and
- subcontractor scopes and responsibilities.

A Project Reclaim skills record may document observed capability, but it cannot substitute for a regulated license, required certification, or qualified contractor.

### 7.3 Participant role boundary — REQUIRED internally

Prepare a role matrix showing:

- tasks participants may perform;
- tasks requiring direct supervision;
- tasks prohibited for unqualified participants;
- training required before each task;
- supervisor-to-worker ratio;
- wage or stipend classification;
- stop-work rights; and
- incident escalation.

Do not describe participants as apprentices unless the relevant apprenticeship program is formally registered or otherwise authorized.

## 8. Environmental, cultural, and regulatory review file

Create `07-Environmental-and-Regulatory`.

At minimum, prepare a screening memo that identifies whether the work may require:

- CEQA review or categorical exemption;
- National Environmental Policy Act review if federal funds or federal land are involved;
- California Coastal Act review where relevant;
- stream, wetland, watercourse, or erosion permits;
- air-quality or prescribed-burning approvals;
- tree-removal or local vegetation permits;
- road-encroachment or traffic controls;
- utility locates;
- cultural-resource or Tribal consultation;
- endangered-species or habitat review;
- waste, chipping, hauling, or disposal rules; and
- county zoning or land-use approval.

The memo should name the responsible reviewer and record each item as `Not Applicable`, `Needs Review`, `Submitted`, `Approved`, or `Blocked`. Do not state that environmental or cultural review is complete without a record from the responsible authority.

## 9. The budget and procurement file

Create `08-Budget-and-Procurement`.

### 9.1 CAL FIRE budget workbook — REQUIRED

Build the budget directly from the treatment specification. Include:

- personnel or contracted labor;
- supervision;
- supplies;
- equipment, separated from supplies;
- transportation and hauling;
- disposal or holding-site costs;
- mapping and professional services;
- insurance and safety costs where allowable;
- outreach or education costs if in scope;
- indirect-cost rate and justification if requested;
- match or in-kind contributions;
- total requested amount; and
- cost per acre, linear foot, parcel, or other output where meaningful.

The FY 2025–26 page recommended requests of $950,000 or less and limited project equipment to a cumulative $750,000. Those limits must be checked again against the next solicitation.

### 9.2 Three-source quote file — STRONGLY RECOMMENDED

For each major cost, retain:

- vendor quote or published price;
- date;
- item or service specification;
- quantity;
- delivery or mobilization cost;
- warranty or maintenance terms;
- insurance implications;
- buy/rent/lease/subcontract analysis; and
- selection rationale.

Do not include a large equipment purchase merely because it appears in the 100-person planning model. Connect every cost to the defined prevention work package and qualified operator.

### 9.3 Equipment disposition plan — REQUIRED where equipment is purchased

Prepare:

- equipment description and serial number field;
- requested funding source;
- intended use;
- qualified operator;
- storage location;
- maintenance owner;
- insurance coverage;
- useful-life estimate;
- disposition rules; and
- post-grant ownership and reporting responsibility.

## 10. The goals and measurement file

Create `09-Goals-and-Evidence`.

### 10.1 Baseline and goals — REQUIRED

For each goal, record:

- baseline value;
- target value;
- unit of measurement;
- data source;
- collection method;
- responsible owner;
- review frequency;
- evidence artifact; and
- public-release status.

Examples:

- treatment acres completed;
- linear feet of access route cleared;
- number of hazardous trees removed where eligible;
- number of properties or parcels treated;
- number of residents reached through education;
- number of safety trainings completed;
- number of work packages accepted by the lead partner; and
- amount of material lawfully hauled, reused, or disposed.

### 10.2 Completion evidence — REQUIRED during performance

Design the evidence workflow before work begins:

- signed work order;
- crew roster or role record;
- daily briefing record;
- dated before/after photographs where authorized;
- GPS or mapped treatment record;
- measured acreage or linear footage;
- material hauling or disposal tickets;
- supervisor inspection;
- incident and near-miss log;
- partner acceptance form; and
- final completion report.

The public portal should report only what has a source, date, owner, reviewer, and release decision.

## 11. The public-benefit and disadvantaged-community file

Create `10-Community-Benefit`.

If the application claims benefit to vulnerable, disadvantaged, or severely disadvantaged communities, retain:

- the exact census tract, community, or service-area basis;
- source for the disadvantaged-community designation;
- population or infrastructure affected;
- documented wildfire-risk or access problem;
- community engagement record;
- language-access and accessibility plan;
- letters from affected community organizations where appropriate;
- explanation of direct benefit; and
- method for avoiding overclaiming participant or community outcomes.

Do not infer disadvantaged-community eligibility solely from poverty, homelessness, incarceration history, Tribal identity, or rural location. Use the designation method required by the solicitation and cite the source.

## 12. The Grants Portal upload package

For a future solicitation, maintain a clean package with:

1. Application form;
2. Scope of Work;
3. Goals;
4. Budget;
5. printable project map PDF;
6. required GIS files or ZIP package;
7. articles of incorporation and nonprofit documentation if applicable;
8. California Standard 204 or current payee form;
9. board resolution or signing authority;
10. commitment letters for claimed cash or in-kind match;
11. support letters, if useful;
12. environmental/regulatory screening record;
13. safety and supervision plan;
14. qualification and insurance records;
15. equipment disposition plan if equipment is proposed; and
16. internal evidence and release plan.

Use the project tracking number assigned by the Grants Portal in filenames after submission. Keep a local manifest that records filename, document type, version, signer, date, checksum, and upload status.

## 13. Current readiness assessment

### Ready to draft

- Project Reclaim has a clear wildfire-resilience purpose.
- The portal has planning, evidence, status, and governance concepts.
- The board packet already separates prevention work from housing, clinical, residential, and broader ecosystem claims.
- The two-county geography provides a starting regional focus.

### Not yet application-ready

- No single CAL FIRE work package has been selected.
- No eligible lead applicant has been confirmed.
- No site-control or right-of-entry record has been confirmed for the proposed treatment area.
- No CAL FIRE-compliant project geopoint and hazardous-fuels treatment polygon have been prepared.
- No site-specific treatment scope, baseline, or cost estimate has been professionally reviewed.
- No executed commitment letter or MOU has been verified.
- No project-specific safety, environmental, cultural, or contractor review has been completed.
- No current grant-cycle deadline is open; the FY 2025–26 Proposition 4 opportunity is closed.

## 14. Recommended order of operations

1. Select one prevention work package, not the whole ecosystem.
2. Identify the eligible lead applicant.
3. Obtain landowner or public-agency authorization.
4. Conduct a site walk and qualified risk assessment.
5. Create the treatment specification and measurable baseline.
6. Resolve environmental, cultural, access, and insurance questions.
7. Produce the geopoint, treatment polygons, and printable map.
8. Obtain commitment letters and comparable quotes.
9. Build the budget, goals, safety plan, and evidence workflow.
10. Have the lead applicant and an independent reviewer check every claim.
11. Monitor CAL FIRE for the next solicitation and re-check every form and limit before submission.
12. Submit only after the package passes the release gate: applicant verified, site authorized, scope measurable, budget supported, map complete, safety assigned, and partner role documented.

## 15. Public claim rule

Until the evidence exists, describe the CAL FIRE work as:

> **Proposed / planning-stage wildfire-prevention work package; applicant, site authorization, mapping, scope, and funding availability pending.**

Do not say:

- CAL FIRE has approved the project;
- Project Reclaim is a CAL FIRE contractor or partner;
- the work is funded;
- acres have been treated;
- a property is authorized;
- a partner is committed; or
- a prevention outcome has been achieved.
