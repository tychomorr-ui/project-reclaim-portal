## First question: who controls the approval?

The address is in the City of Clearlake, Lake County. The City of Clearlake Planning Department provides the city land-use and zoning path, including a city GIS zoning viewer, planning contact, and code-enforcement contact. The city page lists administrative planning contact **707-994-8201** and police/code enforcement **707-994-8251**.[2]

The City of Clearlake Building Department states that a property owner, architect, or licensed contractor submits building-permit applications through the city’s online application portal. Applications are not accepted by mail, email, or over the counter. The city reviews the submission, calculates fees, requests payment, performs plan review, and issues the permit after approval. Approved plans must remain on site for inspection.[3]

## Specific permit and review categories to check

The City of Clearlake Building Department identifies potential fee or review categories including building permits for new construction or remodeling; electrical, mechanical, solar, and roofing permits; school fees; fire mitigation fees; air-quality permits for demolition, dust, asbestos, or mold; water-resource permits for waterfront work; public-water and sewer connections; and new well or septic review through Lake County Environmental Health.[3]

For a Project Reclaim operations hub, the likely review sequence is: first confirm the parcel and zoning; then describe the proposed uses to Planning; then obtain a written determination about allowed uses or a conditional/use permit; then have a licensed design professional or contractor prepare plans; then submit building and related applications through the city portal; then complete plan review, corrections, payment, inspections, and final occupancy approval. This is a planning sequence, not a finding that every permit will be required.

The first practical screening priority is therefore not “buy the biggest listing.” It is to identify properties with enough land and legal-use flexibility for equipment, materials, training, fire-mitigation staging, and future housing, then ask the correct city or county department for a written zoning and permit path. A small mixed-use building may be useful for an office or training room but not for a full operations yard or residential campus.
