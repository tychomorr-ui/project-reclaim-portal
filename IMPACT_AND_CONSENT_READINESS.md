# Project Reclaim Impact, Consent, and Support-Services Readiness

**Status:** Public-information and operating-readiness design; not a clinical protocol, legal advice, or a claim that a service is active.  
**Reference date:** August 12, 2026 (Pacific Time)

## Purpose

Project Reclaim needs to make physical work and community value visible without converting people’s lives, health experiences, or recovery status into marketing content. This readiness model establishes a public dashboard for verifiable operational measures, a consent-first pathway for voluntary participant stories, and a support-services page that distinguishes confirmed services from future qualified-provider pathways.

> **Public principle:** A number is not an impact claim until its source, date, definition, verification status, and calculation method are available for review.

## Public Metric Governance

The landing dashboard should publish only three initial measures: **participants**, **acres restored**, and **local-business partnerships**. Until a source record satisfies the definition below, the public interface should show **“Pending verification”** rather than zero or a made-up count. The CDC evaluation framework emphasizes assessing context, gathering credible evidence, transparency, ethics, and acting on findings; it also distinguishes performance measurement from outcome and impact evaluation.[1] [2]

| Metric | Publishable definition | Minimum evidence | Not a valid substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Participants | Unique people with a documented, voluntary Project Reclaim participation record during the reporting period | De-duplicated record, reporting date, participation definition, data steward approval | An attendance estimate, referral, inquiry, or claimed future capacity |
| Acres restored | Distinct acres within an authorized, documented work scope where the stated restoration activity is complete or in verified progress | Authorized scope or work order, map/geometry or attributable area record, activity type, date, verifier | A service-area estimate, property acreage, proposed work area, or map context layer |
| Local-business partnerships | Local businesses with a documented relationship stage defined by the metric methodology | Signed agreement, executed purchase/contract, documented active vendor relationship, or other stated standard | Form submissions, informal conversations, or businesses listed as prospective contacts |

The dashboard should display a reporting period, “as of” date, methodology link, status badge, and named data steward role. It must not call performance counts “impact” unless the evaluation design supports that conclusion. The CDC notes that outcome evaluation measures the extent to which intended outcomes are achieved but does not by itself establish causality.[1]

## Consent-First Participant Stories

Project Reclaim must never manufacture testimonials, reviews, participant quotes, ratings, photographs, or recovery outcomes. A story can be considered for publication only when it is real, voluntary, approved for the intended channel, and documented through a consent process appropriate to the participant’s circumstances and any involved qualified provider.

| Story gate | Minimum public-facing rule |
|---|---|
| Participation | The person is a real participant or real former participant; no composite stories presented as an individual account |
| Voluntariness | Declining, limiting, or later revoking publication must not affect work, housing, support, benefits, services, or opportunity |
| Scope | Consent names the format, channel, topic, identity level, image/audio use, and expiration or review date |
| Health and recovery privacy | Never publish diagnosis, treatment, substance-use history, therapy details, legal status, or correctional history by default |
| Review | A designated consent/privacy reviewer confirms publication authorization before release |
| Withdrawal | The public site and internal record include a practical withdrawal/retirement process |
| Evidence | Publish only the participant-approved story and verifiable progress facts; retain consent records outside the public site |

When a covered provider or a qualifying substance-use-disorder program is involved, additional privacy rules can apply. California’s DHCS describes consent management and data-sharing guidance for sensitive health, behavioral-health, and social-service information; it also links to federal HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 resources.[3] Project Reclaim should not store clinical records in the public portal and should obtain qualified legal and clinical review before receiving, sharing, or publishing health-related information.

## Support-Services Public Boundary

The support-services page should use three public labels: **Confirmed**, **Partner pathway**, and **Not currently offered**. It can explain the navigation role Project Reclaim intends to play—warm referrals, voluntary care navigation, transportation/logistics coordination where available, life-skills learning, and participant support planning—without claiming to deliver mental-health treatment, substance-use treatment, therapy, medical care, housing, or justice supervision unless a confirmed, qualified program actually exists.

DHCS identifies enhanced care management as a Medi-Cal managed-care benefit that may coordinate health and health-related services for qualifying members, and notes Community Supports that address certain health-related social needs.[3] These are potential coordination concepts, not evidence that Project Reclaim is an ECM provider, Community Supports provider, or clinical service organization.

## Upgrade Roadmap

| Priority | Upgrade | Why it matters | Release condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Metric registry and evidence ledger | Turns public claims into auditable records | Metric definitions, source roles, approval workflow, and reporting cadence are approved |
| 2 | Participant consent vault outside the public portal | Protects real stories and withdrawal rights | Counsel/privacy review, consent forms, role access, retention schedule, and provider coordination are approved |
| 3 | Referral and care-navigation directory | Connects people to verified local support without pretending to provide clinical care | Directory owner, partner verification, escalation protocol, and update cadence are assigned |
| 4 | Work-readiness and credential ledger | Makes training, safety qualifications, and work milestones portable and visible to the participant | Credential standards, participant access, corrections process, and privacy review are complete |
| 5 | Project operations desk | Connects property assessment, work orders, crews, equipment, materials, costs, and evidence | Authorized project scopes and operational owners are in place |
| 6 | Local procurement and vendor qualification workflow | Converts business interest into fair, documented purchasing and subcontracting processes | Procurement policy, conflict-of-interest controls, insurance/licensing review, and bid process are approved |
| 7 | Community partner intake and agreement tracker | Keeps prospective relationships distinct from active agreements | Partner category schema, agreement review path, and status definitions are approved |

## Professional Review Checklist

Before publishing any confirmed metric, story, or support-service statement, the designated reviewer should document that the claim has a data source, approved methodology, appropriate consent/privacy basis, and a current accountable owner. Before receiving protected health, substance-use, corrections, or other sensitive data, Project Reclaim should obtain legal counsel and qualified clinical/privacy review.
