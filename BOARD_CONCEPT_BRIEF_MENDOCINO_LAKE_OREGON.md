## Executive proposition

Project Reclaim is a physical-world initiative designed to connect land stewardship, fire-resilience work, wood reclamation, workforce development, residential readiness, and evidence-backed community value. The first pilot is proposed as a **two-site hybrid**: a vineyard-style residential and land-stewardship campus, paired with a Clearlake-area operations, materials, and field-school hub centered on the public lead at **3360 Old Highway 53**. Both locations are planning candidates only. No property is secured, no purchase is recommended, and no public agency or partner is represented as committed.

The board is not being asked to approve a statewide system on day one. It is being asked to support a disciplined path toward **one lawful, supervised, measurable pilot** that can prove whether underused property, practical training, local procurement, and authorized resilience work can reinforce one another.

## Why this model

Mendocino and Lake County provide the planning context for a model that addresses several connected challenges: unused or underused property, housing instability, reentry and workforce barriers, wildfire exposure, rural transportation constraints, and the need for practical land and property stewardship. The project’s response is not to promise that a large number of people can be deployed immediately. It is to build trained capacity through stable housing or community-based participation, supervised work, qualified instruction, partner referrals, and verifiable portfolios.

The long-term model can include people entering through walk-in workforce pathways, veterans’ pathways, homelessness and housing referrals, recovery and behavioral-health partner referrals, reentry or probation/parole referrals where lawfully structured, and ordinary career transition. A person’s referral source must never be treated as a substitute for rights, safety, informed participation, or qualified professional oversight.

An operations hub can concentrate on tool control, materials, procurement, field preparation, and supervised production. Transportation, insurance, staffing, and site-authority requirements must be solved before the sites are connected operationally.

## First pilot cohort and operating concept

The planning cohort is **12 residential participants plus up to 8 community-based participants**, subject to property capacity, lawful referrals, staffing, insurance, and safety review. The number is a planning target, not a promised enrollment or outcome. The program would offer a one-year foundational route, a two-year advancement route, and future four- or five-year apprenticeships in carpentry, forestry, fire resilience, disaster preparation, property rehabilitation, equipment and logistics, and community operations.

The first year would follow four operating seasons. In the first 90 days, the team would establish property controls, habitability and fire/life-safety review, intake and rights information, transportation, food, tool inventory, emergency contacts, and referral pathways. During months four through six, participants would complete supervised low-risk rehabilitation and field-school work such as weatherization, painting, hand-tool carpentry, grounds, storage, tool control, and documentation. During months seven through nine, approved work could expand into wood recovery, vegetation and fire-resilience support, drainage or storm-preparation work, and partner-defined community projects. During months ten through twelve, participants would complete portfolios, pursue partner-issued credentials, transition toward employment or apprenticeship, and evaluate whether a second property is justified.

The operating team would require a program director, residential or site manager, construction/facilities supervisor, field-stewardship supervisor, safety lead, referral or case-coordination role, administrative and data support, and qualified external providers. Registered Apprenticeship should be pursued through industry and state or federal apprenticeship channels rather than self-awarding a Project Reclaim credential. The U.S. Department of Labor describes Registered Apprenticeship as paid work with progressive wage increases, structured on-the-job learning, supplemental education, and portable credentials validated by the Department or a State Apprenticeship Agency [4].

## Disaster-readiness relationship

Project Reclaim could prepare people and property for authorized fire, flood, storm, river, drainage, restoration, and recovery work. It would not present itself as an emergency-management agency or independently deploy people into an incident. Oregon’s State Fire Marshal describes wildfire preparedness as a coordinated effort involving state, local, and federal partners and supports preparedness and response programs [5]. FEMA’s BRIC program illustrates why mitigation work should be framed around eligible public, Tribal, state, and local applicants and hazard-mitigation outcomes rather than an unsupported private emergency claim [6].

The first pilot should therefore emphasize seasonal readiness: tool and PPE readiness, defensible-space and vegetation work where authorized, drainage and erosion preparation, access and staging, materials recovery, documented work orders, and trained supervision. Any fire suppression, hotshot, floodwater, utility, structural, medical, law-enforcement, or incident-command role requires separate qualification and agency authority.

## What the board is being asked to authorize

The immediate request should be divided into five decisions. First, authorize a property-search and due-diligence workstream for one residential or lodging candidate and one operations/shop candidate. Second, authorize partner conversations with county planning, building and safety, fire-related offices, workforce organizations, property owners, local businesses, qualified providers, and relevant justice or reentry agencies. Third, authorize development of a lawful pilot operating manual covering participant rights, safety, work classification, supervision, referral boundaries, data governance, and release conditions. Fourth, authorize a staged procurement process based on approved work scopes rather than buying the entire 100-person package at once. Fifth, authorize preparation of a funding package that separates site costs, people and supervision, participant support, tools and equipment, evidence and governance, and contingency.

## Measures of readiness and success

The first-year proof should focus on evidence, not inflated reach. The board should expect a source-backed record of property status, authorized work, staffing, participant agreements, training milestones, completed work packages, credentials actually issued by qualified partners, local purchasing, materials reclaimed, safety observations, and transition outcomes. Public metrics should remain pending until verified records exist. Participant stories should remain unpublished unless consent is explicit, documented, and revocable.
