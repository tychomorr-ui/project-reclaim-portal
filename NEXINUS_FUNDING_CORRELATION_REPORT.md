# NEXINUS Funding Correlation Report
## Prepared for Tyler Morris

**Research/status date:** November 13, 2026
**Purpose:** Translate the verified funding-lane results into an actionable funding strategy for Project Reclaim and Pharo-Lapse without overstating eligibility, availability, deadlines, award amounts, partners, deployments, participants, or impact.

## Executive summary

The strongest near-term opportunity is **not a grant for Project Reclaim’s full operating model**. Project Reclaim is currently described as a California workforce, land-stewardship, wood-recovery, training, housing-readiness, and partner-mobilization pilot. That core model is a poor match for open-internet, AI-product, internet-freedom, foundational-software, or general technology funds.

The most credible funding lanes are therefore **narrow, component-specific, and mostly future-cycle or conditional**:

1. **CAL FIRE Wildfire Prevention Grants** are the strongest substantive match for a future, mapped, authorized wildfire-prevention work package in Mendocino or Lake County, provided an eligible public, Tribal, special-district, Fire Safe Council, or qualified 501(c)(3) applicant leads. The FY 2025–26 cycle is closed.
2. **DOL RESTART** is the strongest thematic match for Project Reclaim’s reentry and workforce elements, but the 2026 competition is closed and the program’s scale and applicant structure exceed the current local pilot.
3. **FEMA BRIC** may fit a future designed public infrastructure hazard-mitigation project, but not the broader workforce, housing, land-stewardship, or operating model. The FY 2024–25 cycle is closed.
4. **NLnet Restack, Mozilla Human-Scale AI, OTF, and Mozilla Builders** are plausible only for a genuinely separate technical product with documented users or pilots, technical deliverables, public/open outputs, and the applicable geography, legal, security, or charitable-purpose requirements. Of these, **NLnet Restack** and **Mozilla Human-Scale AI** have confirmed current deadlines, but both require immediate evidence work and narrow technical proposals.
5. **Sovereign Tech Agency** is a weak-to-conditional lead for Pharo-Lapse only if it is mature, widely used, openly licensed foundational software or a qualifying security-maintenance project. No fixed deadline or award amount is confirmed.

**Pharo-Lapse cannot be responsibly ranked as eligible or aligned** because no project brief, repository, product description, legal status, geography, team information, user evidence, license, or technical documentation was supplied. Every Pharo-Lapse conclusion below is conditional.

## 2. Strongest near-term matches

### A. CAL FIRE: future wildfire-prevention package

This is the clearest strategic match to Project Reclaim’s geography and proposed field activities, but **the verified FY 2025–26 opportunity is closed**. A future application should be a discrete California wildfire-prevention project—not a request for the full Reclaim model.

A credible package could involve mapped hazardous-fuels reduction, qualifying defensible-space support, evacuation/access work, wildfire-prevention planning, risk mapping, or public education. The work should be supervised and tied to measurable prevention outputs. Participant wages and training may support delivery, but housing-readiness, treatment/referral services, general operations, and unrestricted participant support should not be presented as the grant’s primary outputs.

Project Reclaim must first confirm whether it has an eligible 501(c)(3) status. If not, it should identify an eligible county, city, Tribal Nation, special district, Fire Safe Council, resource-conservation district, or other qualified nonprofit lead. CAL FIRE’s rules prohibit assuming that a private or for-profit entity can apply directly or use an informal pass-through structure.

### B. DOL RESTART: future reentry/workforce pathway

RESTART is the strongest thematic match for supervised paid work, skilled field work, credentials, reentry referrals, pre-apprenticeship, and Registered Apprenticeship exploration. However, the 2026 FOA is closed, and the current local cohort concept is much smaller than the FOA’s enrollment scale. A future route would likely require an eligible state, Tribal, or qualifying national/regional intermediary lead and a broader multi-region delivery structure.

Project Reclaim should preserve its careful language: participants must not be called apprentices unless the relevant program is formally registered or otherwise authorized. Before any successor application, establish employer commitments, training and apprenticeship relationships, participant definitions, enrollment capacity, performance measurement, and a compliant sponsor/subgrantee structure.

### C. FEMA BRIC: designed mitigation infrastructure only

BRIC could become relevant if a county, city, special district, or Tribal partner sponsors a defined infrastructure hazard-mitigation project. The current packet does not establish conceptual design, site control, permits, hazard-plan consistency, environmental/historic review, or benefit-cost/effectiveness evidence.

A future BRIC concept should be separated from Project Reclaim’s workforce and residential model. It should identify the public infrastructure or risk-reduction asset, the responsible public applicant, the hazard addressed, the design stage, the matching plan, and the measurable reduction in risk to people, property, or critical services.

### D. NLnet Restack: only a standalone open technical component

NLnet is a near-term deadline, but not a near-term match for the full Reclaim budget. A viable submission would need to isolate a real open-internet technical project—such as privacy-preserving participant data governance, local-first/offline field evidence, provenance and chain-of-custody tooling, or a reusable secure data workflow—and release all software/hardware results under a recognized free/open-source license.

The application must also address the Restack European-dimension requirement. A US-only team should not infer eligibility from the general application form’s global country list. A qualifying European team receiving at least 60% of funds is the ordinary route; an exceptional technical-expertise case is not a general waiver.

The first request should be a technically bounded work package, generally €5,000–€50,000, with milestones, repository plan, license, security/privacy rationale, and value to users beyond Project Reclaim alone. It should not include land, vehicles, tools, insurance, supervision, housing, treatment, or general pilot costs.

## 3. Possible but conditional matches

### Mozilla Human-Scale AI Incubator

This is a live call with a confirmed November 20, 2026 deadline, but it is suitable only for a working AI product with users or pilots, technical implementation, a charitable public-benefit purpose, and capacity to participate in the cohort. Project Reclaim’s board packet does not prove that such a product exists.

A possible Reclaim framing would be a privacy-preserving, community-governed, local/offline participant work-portfolio or evidence system. That framing is not enough by itself. Before applying, Reclaim would need to show a working prototype already used or piloted by intended users, product-community fit, a primarily technical plan, minimized and controlled sensitive data, and an open/public output plan. It would also need a lawful US 501(c)(3) funding recipient or fiscal sponsor.

Pharo-Lapse could be a better candidate than Reclaim if it is a working AI system with documented users, efficient or local operation, meaningful user control, and a charitable public benefit. None of those facts has been supplied or verified.

### OTF Internet Freedom Fund

OTF’s rolling IFF concept-note route is actionable in form but conditional in substance. It is for technology addressing censorship, surveillance, secure communications, privacy, digital security, or shutdown resilience in repressive environments. A general portal, local infrastructure tool, or workforce platform is not enough.

Pharo-Lapse should be assessed against a threat model, target users and geographies, public repository, open-source license, active development, security plan, and concrete technical milestones. Project Reclaim should not submit its current physical pilot, radios, or evidence workflow as an OTF proposal.

OTF’s FOSS Sustainability and Surge and Sustain mechanisms are currently closed. Rapid Response is for acute, time-sensitive digital emergencies, which the supplied Reclaim packet does not describe.

### Sovereign Tech Fund and Resilience

This lane is conditional for a mature, publicly maintained, openly licensed foundational technology or for qualifying security-hardening work. It is not a prototype or user-facing application fund. No fixed award amount or deadline is confirmed, and the regular Fund requires estimated work above €50,000.

Pharo-Lapse would need a canonical repository, license, adoption/dependency evidence, maintainer endorsement, backlog, and a workplan focused on foundational maintenance or security. Project Reclaim has no evidence of such a component. The EU Pilot is not a confirmed open application call as of the research date.

Do not represent the historical terms as available funding.

The current Reclaim project is not an internet-freedom or digital-security project. Its California location, workforce model, land work, and community goals do not establish OTF fit. A future standalone security-critical open-source product would need to be assessed separately.

### Sovereign Tech Agency for current Project Reclaim

The current project is not a foundational open digital technology. Its proposed portal and evidence workflow are program tools, not evidence of broad adoption, maintainership, or infrastructure significance.

### NLnet for the full Reclaim budget

NLnet is not a general-purpose operating, social-service, land-stewardship, housing, workforce, or capital-equipment fund. The stated Project Reclaim budget range of $150,000–$250,000 for first-stage work and $450,000–$600,000 for a controlled pilot should not be submitted as a Restack request.

## 5. Recommended funding narrative

### Project Reclaim narrative

> **Project Reclaim is a partner-led California wildfire-resilience and reentry-workforce model that converts hazardous-fuels and land-stewardship work into supervised, documented pathways toward lawful paid employment, credentials, and community safety.** The initial funding request will support one tightly defined, authorized work package—not the entire residential, clinical, housing, or expansion model. The package will be selected with the responsible public, Tribal, fire, county, or land-management partner; mapped to a specific site and hazard; governed by written access and safety controls; and measured through completed prevention outputs, worker training and safety records, and verified partner acceptance. Sensitive participant information will be minimized and access-controlled. Any future data or software component will be separately scoped, openly licensed where required, and funded only by a technology-appropriate program.

Use this narrative for CAL FIRE or a future public-sector wildfire-prevention lane after confirming the applicant and site. Adapt it for a future DOL workforce opportunity by emphasizing reentry eligibility, paid work-based learning, credentials, employer commitments, and authorized apprenticeship pathways. Do not use it unchanged for NLnet, Mozilla, OTF, or Sovereign Tech Agency.

### Pharo-Lapse narrative: conditional template only

Because no verified Pharo-Lapse description was supplied, the following is a template, not a factual description:

> **Pharo-Lapse is a [working/open-source/foundational/local-AI/internet-freedom] technical project that addresses [verified user problem] for [verified users/geographies].** Its current implementation is [repository/product evidence], licensed under [verified license], and has [verified users, deployments, dependencies, or pilot evidence]. The proposed work will deliver [specific technical milestones], publish [verified public outputs], and improve [privacy, security, resilience, interoperability, or user control] while minimizing sensitive data. The applicant/funding recipient is [verified legal entity or fiscal sponsor], and the team satisfies [verified geography, charitable-purpose, European-dimension, or other program-specific condition].

Do not fill the brackets with assumptions. The absence of a project brief is currently the primary blocker to any responsible Pharo-Lapse recommendation.

## 6. What to prepare before applying

### Common evidence package

- Confirm the legal applicant, tax status, fiscal sponsor if needed, SAM/UEI or other registration requirements, and authority to receive funds.
- Prepare a one-page project brief that separates the fundable work package from the broader organizational vision.
- Establish site control, partner authority, permits, insurance, safety responsibilities, and written roles for any field project.
- Build a milestone budget that distinguishes allowable direct costs from general operations, housing, capital equipment, participant support, and unsupported expansion costs.
- Create a risk register covering participant safety, land/property diligence, data minimization, consent, privacy, security, environmental review, and partner dependencies.
- Document what is already working. Do not call a concept a prototype, a user a participant, a partner a committed partner, or a planned deployment an existing deployment.

### Technology-specific evidence

- Canonical repository URL, commit history, documentation, issue tracker, license for every software/hardware output, and third-party dependency inventory.
- Technical architecture, threat model, security plan, privacy/data-flow diagram, reproducibility plan, and concrete release milestones.
- Verified users, pilots, adoption, dependencies, or deployment evidence; distinguish current evidence from targets.
- Clear decision on whether outputs are open source, open hardware, open access, or merely publicly documented.
- Team geography and legal status, including the NLnet European-dimension analysis and any Mozilla/OTF/Sovereign Tech requirements.

### Reclaim-specific evidence

- Confirm whether Project Reclaim is a 501(c)(3), another eligible nonprofit, or neither; identify the actual applicant for each lane.
- Obtain written county, Tribal, fire, landowner, special-district, or other lead-partner commitments rather than describing prospective relationships as partnerships.
- Select one site and one work package; verify authorization, parcel/site control, hazard mapping, fire-threat status, environmental and historic review needs, conceptual design, and cost estimate.
- Define participant eligibility, ages, justice-involvement criteria, wages, supervision, training providers, employer partners, credential issuers, and apprenticeship status.
- Separate wildfire-prevention outputs from reentry/workforce outcomes and from housing, treatment, referral, and general support services.
- Create a defensible baseline and measurement plan. No impact metric, participant count, deployment count, or outcome should be reported until it is actually documented.

### Pharo-Lapse evidence request

Before any application decision, obtain: project description; product/repository URL; current version and users; legal applicant; country/team geography; open-source license; architecture; threat model; privacy and security controls; deployment or pilot evidence; dependencies/adoption; proposed milestones; budget; and any maintainer, fiscal sponsor, or public-benefit documentation. Until then, classify Pharo-Lapse as **unknown**, not eligible.

## 7. 30-day application plan

### Days 1–3: triage and evidence freeze

1. Assign one owner for Project Reclaim and one for Pharo-Lapse.
2. Freeze unsupported claims in all drafts: nonprofit status, open-source status, partners, deployments, participant counts, deadlines, award amounts, and impact metrics.
3. Obtain the missing Pharo-Lapse dossier and create a yes/no evidence checklist.
4. Decide whether the immediate priority is the Mozilla deadline, the NLnet deadline, or preparation for future CAL FIRE/DOL/FEMA cycles. Do not submit multiple mismatched applications simply because deadlines exist.

### Days 4–7: applicant and fit gates

1. Verify Reclaim’s legal entity and identify eligible lead applicants for CAL FIRE and FEMA.
2. Identify whether any real Reclaim or Pharo-Lapse technical product satisfies Mozilla, NLnet, OTF, or Sovereign Tech criteria.
3. For Mozilla, confirm working-product evidence, users/pilots, charitable purpose, US funding recipient/fiscal sponsor, and ability to participate.
4. For NLnet, confirm the technical scope, recognized license, public repository plan, European team/funding share, and request size.
5. For OTF and Sovereign Tech, stop unless the technical/security/foundational evidence is real and documented.

### Days 8–14: build the selected package

1. Draft a two-page problem statement, theory of change, milestones, budget, risks, and evidence table.
2. For Reclaim wildfire work, obtain a map, site authorization, partner letter, prevention scope, safety plan, and preliminary cost estimate.
3. For a technical proposal, prepare architecture, license, threat model, public-output plan, and user evidence.
4. For a workforce proposal, document employer/training/apprenticeship commitments and a scalable participant model.
5. Have an independent reviewer mark every factual claim as verified, conditional, or unsupported.

### Days 15–21: application drafting and compliance

1. Draft the narrowest eligible application; exclude unrelated program costs.
2. Reconcile budget lines to the selected program’s cost and award rules without treating historical ceilings as current availability.
3. Confirm all required registrations, attachments, English-language requirements, fiscal sponsorship, public-sector approvals, and submission portals.
4. Re-check official program pages immediately before submission, especially Mozilla’s November 20, 2026 deadline and NLnet’s January 3, 2027 deadline.

### Days 22–30: decision and submission

1. Submit only if the fit gate is passed and all required evidence is in hand.
2. If evidence is missing, send an eligibility inquiry or partner-development request rather than a speculative application.
3. For closed programs, set monitoring actions for official successor notices; do not advertise them as available funding.
4. Archive the submitted version, source URLs, date checked, assumptions, and unresolved risks.

## 8. Explicit warnings and decision rules

- **Current status:** CAL FIRE FY 2025–26, FEMA FY 2024–25 BRIC, DOL RESTART 2026, and Mozilla Builders’ documented Accelerator cycle are closed or historical. Do not describe them as open.
- **Deadlines:** The confirmed current deadlines are Mozilla Human-Scale AI on November 20, 2026 at 11:59 p.m. Pacific and NLnet Restack on January 3, 2027 at 12:00 CET. No later or recurring deadline should be inferred.
- **Award amounts:** Historical or published ranges are not promises. Mozilla’s additional $200,000 is conditional; OTF’s range is a mechanism parameter; NLnet awards are competitive; Sovereign Tech has no fixed amount on the reviewed page; closed-cycle FEMA, CAL FIRE, and DOL figures are not current awards.
- **Nonprofit status:** None of these findings establishes that Project Reclaim or Pharo-Lapse is a 501(c)(3). Several lanes permit nontraditional applicants, while CAL FIRE and other mechanisms impose specific applicant rules. Verify before applying.
- **Open-source licensing:** Open-source status is required or central for NLnet, Mozilla Builders, and certain OTF/Sovereign Tech routes, but is not a universal requirement and cannot be assumed for either project.
- **Eligibility:** A global application form does not override a program-specific European, sanctions, public-sector, geographic, age, applicant, or charitable-purpose condition.
- **Partners and deployments:** Prospective partners, planned sites, proposed pilots, and intended deployments are not confirmed partners, authorized sites, existing users, or current deployments.
- **Impact metrics:** No participant count, outcome, user count, adoption figure, safety result, or impact metric should be invented or presented as achieved without source evidence.
- **Project separation:** Do not use a technology component to make the broader Project Reclaim operating model appear technology-eligible. Submit only a genuinely standalone, technically justified work package.
