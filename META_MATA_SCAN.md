# Project Reclaim META-MATA

## Multidimensional Market Analysis and Opportunistic Scan

**Reference date:** August 12, 2026 (Pacific Time)  
**Working geography:** Northern California, with Lake County as an initial contextual reference—not a published service-area commitment.  
**Purpose:** Identify practical, ethical, and partnership-ready expansion pathways for Project Reclaim without representing prospective sites, funders, contracts, or organizations as active relationships.

> **Operating premise:** Project Reclaim should create voluntary, paid, skill-building pathways into useful physical work. Housing, treatment, behavioral-health support, case management, and legal needs must be delivered through qualified partners and participant choice—not substituted by a work program.

| Signal | What the source establishes | Project Reclaim implication | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forest-sector business and workforce support | CAL FIRE’s Wood Products and Bioenergy Program states that its Business and Workforce Development Grants support forest-sector business operations, workforce skills, and innovation. [1] | A materials-to-value and worker-development pathway is a credible funding and partnership conversation, subject to current guidelines and eligibility. | High |
| Community wildfire prevention | CAL FIRE’s Wildfire Prevention Grants describe locally led risk-reduction work including fuel reduction, defensible space, roadside work, community chipping, mapping, education, and selected equipment. [2] | The portal’s map, work-order, evidence, and local-crew model tracks recognizable public-prevention activity categories. | High |
| Local workforce precedent | Workforce Alliance of the North Bay describes Lake County wildfire-resilience training in partnership with Tribal EcoRestoration Alliance, including training, certifications, paid work experience, and OJT pathways. [3] | Project Reclaim should present itself as complementary infrastructure and must not claim participation in that program unless confirmed. | High |
| Reentry workforce support | California’s HIRE 2.0 program describes training, reskilling, supportive services, and needs-related payments for justice-involved people; the 2026 opportunity was limited to existing HIRE organizations and required matching resources. [4] | The near-term move is partnership with eligible workforce and reentry organizations, rather than claiming a standalone HIRE application route. | High |
| Housing and employment linkage | HUD Exchange describes sustainable employment as a key pathway out of homelessness and provides resources on workforce and housing-system partnerships. [5] | Project Reclaim should separate employment delivery from housing navigation while designing a warm-referral and coordinated-entry partner layer. | High |
| Supportive-service structure | HUD’s CoC supportive-service guidance lists case management, employment assistance, job training, life skills, mental-health services, substance-use treatment, transportation, and housing search among eligible categories subject to program rules. [6] | The initiative needs a partner-enabled support matrix, clear consent, data minimization, and boundaries around clinical and housing services. | High |

## Opportunity Matrix

| Ecosystem lane | Opportunity to build | First credible artifact | Safeguard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fire resilience | Assessment-to-work-order workflow for defensible space, roadside fuels, evacuation access, and material recovery. | Site intake, crew scope, before/after evidence checklist, verification status. | Use licensed/qualified oversight and local permitting; do not publish private site details without consent. |
| Reclaimed wood | Sort recovered wood into reuse, milling stock, firewood, chips, biomass, or disposal pathways. | Material passport with source, condition, species if known, handling requirements, and reuse route. | Treat tool use, dust control, structural reuse, and resale as safety/quality programs—not informal assumptions. |
| Hand-tool craft studio | Teach repair, furniture, finish carpentry, and small-product work using reclaimed material. | Curriculum ladder: safety → measurement → repair → joinery → finishing → portfolio. | Maintain supervised shop standards, accessible participation, and a clear boundary between training and uncompensated production. |
| Site renewal | Assess abandoned rural or in-town properties for stewardship, rehabilitation, housing feasibility, community use, or material recovery. | Due-diligence checklist: title, zoning, utilities, contamination, fire risk, structural condition, permits, accessibility, and stewardship plan. | Do not imply that a property is available, purchasable, or suitable before legal and technical review. |
| Workforce reintegration | Create an opt-in pathway for returning residents, people experiencing homelessness, people in recovery, and people managing mental-health barriers. | Individualized pathway: welcome → paid orientation → safety → skills plan → paid field/shop work → credential/portfolio → placement or advancement. | Participation must be voluntary, non-coercive, paid where productive work is performed, and paired with qualified support referrals. |
| Local procurement | Route spend to local tree services, contractors, arborists, nurseries, tool dealers, equipment sellers/renters, haulers, builders, and repair shops. | Local supplier registry with service category, geography, insurance/certification fields, fair-bid notes, and relationship status. | Avoid exclusivity, conflicts of interest, and unverified supplier claims. |
| Partner mesh | Define what agencies, Tribes, workforce boards, contractors, landholders, and housing/service organizations each contribute. | Partnership intake with organization category, requested role, geography, project type, and readiness stage. | Name outreach audiences precisely, but never imply endorsement, funding, authority, or partnership. |

## Near-Term Design Decisions

1. **Map before claims.** Build the interactive map and its underlying schema now, while leaving pins empty until a property, landholder, or project record is approved for publication.
2. **Warm handoffs, not forced placement.** The workforce story should offer paid orientation, skill development, and referrals; it must not suggest that people should be routed into labor as a condition of housing, supervision, treatment, or basic support.
3. **Pilot one value loop.** A practical first loop is: fuels reduction or recovery → material sorting → supervised hand-tool studio → local repair/furniture/building components → documented community benefit.
4. **Separate decision rights.** Land acquisition, state contracting, housing, clinical services, and workforce operations have distinct legal, insurance, safety, and governance requirements. The portal should make those stages visible rather than collapsing them into one promise.
5. **Use a partner-enabled operating model.** The fastest credible first deployment is likely a coordinated model with qualified forestry, contractor, workforce, housing, behavioral-health, and stewardship partners—each accountable for their own scope.
