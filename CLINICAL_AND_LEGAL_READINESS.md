# Project Reclaim Clinical, Legal, and Geospatial Readiness Matrix

> **Working analysis — not legal or medical advice.** I’m an AI, not a lawyer or medical professional. This is a program-design and due-diligence framework; California counsel, a licensed clinical leader, qualified privacy counsel, insurance professionals, and the relevant local agencies must review it before Project Reclaim offers, advertises, contracts for, stores, or coordinates regulated services.

**Reference date:** August 12, 2026 (Pacific Time)  
**Purpose:** Keep Project Reclaim’s physical-work and workforce mission moving while separating what may be built now from clinical, corrections, housing, privacy, and geospatial capabilities that require qualified partners, licenses, data rights, or formal agreements.

## Executive Direction

Project Reclaim can immediately operate as a **nonclinical workforce, stewardship, material-recovery, and local-procurement initiative**. Its safest first model is a partner-enabled network: participants voluntarily enter paid work and skill pathways; licensed providers independently deliver therapy, clinical behavioral-health care, substance-use treatment, medication management, and case management; and public agencies participate only under executed agreements.

DHCS states that it has sole authority to license facilities providing residential nonmedical services to eligible adults recovering from alcohol or other drug misuse or abuse. It also identifies detoxification, individual or group sessions, educational sessions, recovery or treatment planning, and incidental medical services as triggers for licensing analysis. [1] A Project Reclaim site therefore must **not** describe itself as a treatment, detoxification, therapy, or co-occurring-disorder facility until its legal structure, provider roles, premises, licensing path, and local approvals are confirmed.

| Operating posture | What Project Reclaim may responsibly say | What it must not claim yet | Readiness requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workforce and stewardship hub | “Paid work, training, mentorship, material recovery, and warm referrals are being developed.” | That it provides therapy, treatment, detoxification, clinical case management, or medical care. | Workforce policies, safety program, participant consent, employment/contracting structure, insurance, and partner directory. |
| Partner-hosted care pathway | “Qualified external providers may offer services under their own licensure and agreements.” | That Project Reclaim clinicians diagnose or treat participants unless it actually employs/engages qualified providers in a compliant model. | Executed MOU/service agreement, scope separation, privacy/data-sharing protocol, credential verification, referral workflow. |
| Licensed or regulated program | “The program provides specific regulated services” only after approval. | Any treatment-facility, therapy, or licensed residential-service representation before authorization. | California counsel, licensing analysis, DHCS/local approvals as applicable, clinical governance, privacy/security, policies, records, insurance, and staffing. |

## Clinical and Recovery Partnership Model

The recommended first-stage design is **co-occurring support coordination**, not independent co-occurring treatment. It should make clear that people may receive integrated support from qualified providers, while Project Reclaim supplies the physical-work pathway, voluntary training, employment supports, and—only where appropriate—consent-based warm handoffs.

| Function | Project Reclaim role | Qualified partner role | Minimum guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mental-health care and therapy | Explain options, request permission for a referral, and maintain a nonclinical attendance/support boundary. | Licensed clinicians and behavioral-health organizations assess, diagnose, treat, document, supervise, and bill for care. | Verify licensure through California’s DCA/BBS lookup and establish written scope boundaries. [3] |
| Substance-use disorder support | Offer nonclinical support and referrals; do not collect treatment details in general program forms. | DHCS-licensed/certified providers deliver SUD treatment, recovery planning, and regulated residential/outpatient services. | Analyze licensure before services are offered; use a separate consent/records process when applicable. [1] |
| Crisis and safety | Maintain a site safety and emergency-response policy; provide emergency routing and trained de-escalation procedures within role limits. | Crisis teams, emergency services, clinicians, and county behavioral-health systems provide clinical intervention. | Never represent an unlicensed workforce site as a crisis-treatment setting. |
| Housing and reentry navigation | Provide referral navigation with participant permission. | Housing, Continuum of Care, reentry, managed-care, and justice-involved service partners provide eligibility, enrollment, and funded services. | No coercive labor-for-housing arrangement; document voluntary participation and distinct service agreements. |

California’s Justice-Involved Reentry Initiative is a concrete coordination reference: it enables eligible people who are incarcerated to enroll in Medi-Cal and receive a targeted set of services in the 90 days before release, emphasizing continuity between correctional and community care. [2] Project Reclaim should position itself as a **potential community workforce destination and referral partner**, not as the provider of those Medi-Cal services unless it later earns that status through the appropriate contracts and compliance path.

## Privacy and Data Boundaries

Project Reclaim’s public portal and basic partner forms should collect only business contact information, high-level project context, and non-sensitive workforce interest. They should not solicit diagnoses, treatment histories, medications, probation details, case files, or private property information.

| Data category | Default policy | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Public project, product, or partner information | Publish only after authorization and verification. | Protects landholders, partners, participants, and public trust. |
| Employment/training interest | Store only the minimum data needed to follow up; use a separate, access-controlled process for sensitive support needs. | Limits unnecessary risk and overcollection. |
| Health and treatment information | Do not collect in general portal forms or nonclinical databases. | HIPAA applies to covered entities/business associates and sets safeguards/limits for protected health information. [4] |
| SUD treatment records | Do not request, upload, or expose them through workforce tools. | Part 2 protects records of federally assisted SUD diagnosis, treatment, or referral programs and controls disclosure. [5] |
| Corrections/probation/parole information | Collect only under an executed agreement, a defined lawful purpose, role-based access, and participant protections. | Sensitive justice information requires agency-approved data governance; an outreach relationship is not data-sharing authorization. |

The HIPAA Privacy Rule establishes national protections for individually identifiable health information handled by covered entities and certain business associates. [4] Part 2 applies additional confidentiality protections to qualifying SUD records and, as of February 16, 2026, its updated compliance regime is in effect. [5] These requirements mean a generic customer-relationship system, project registry, or public map is not an acceptable place to hold clinical or treatment records.

## Probation, Parole, Justice, and Corrections Readiness

Project Reclaim can build an outreach pathway for probation departments, parole providers, county reentry staff, CDCR-aligned organizations, justice-focused community organizations, and federal/local justice stakeholders. It must not say it is “partnered” with any of them until a responsible official and Project Reclaim execute the relevant agreement.

| Readiness item | Required before formal participant referrals or data exchange |
|---|---|
| Shared purpose | Define the voluntary workforce, training, placement, or site-stewardship objective; prohibit coercive labor conditions. |
| Agency agreement | Written MOU or contract describing referrals, eligibility, role boundaries, reporting, termination, and liability. |
| Data governance | Data map, legal basis, consent/authorization standard, minimum necessary fields, access control, retention, breach response, and prohibition on using treatment records against participants. |
| Participant protections | Clear grievance channel, nonretaliation policy, reasonable accommodations process, anti-discrimination controls, and a choice not to participate. |
| Employment design | Wage/worker-classification review, workers’ compensation, safety training, supervision, transportation plan, tool/equipment policy, and a documented advancement path. |
| Oversight | Named accountable owner, partner review cadence, incident protocol, and external legal/clinical review. |

## Geospatial and Property-Context Readiness

OnX Hunt is a proprietary commercial product. Project Reclaim should not scrape, republish, or imply access to OnX data without the relevant license, permitted integration, and terms review. Instead, the portal should use a **source-adapter approach**: each overlay is registered with its source, license/terms, refresh date, geographic precision, visibility rules, and authorization status.

| Overlay type | Readiness approach | Source direction |
|---|---|---|
| Project site pins | Keep coordinates private until landholder/agency authorization; distinguish proposed, active, and verified records. | Project Reclaim’s own structured registry. |
| BLM/public-land context | Add only an authorized overlay with attribution, metadata, and no claim that it establishes title, access, or permission. | BLM’s Geospatial Business Platform Hub provides national/state data and services in formats including GeoJSON and GeoServices. [6] |
| California public data | Use the California State Geoportal to locate authoritative boundary, land-use, environmental, and emergency data services; record source terms and refresh dates. | California State Geoportal. [7] |
| Parcel boundaries/ownership | Use county assessor/GIS or licensed parcel vendors after local accuracy, usage, and redistribution terms are reviewed. | County-specific authoritative sources or a licensed provider; never treat a visual line as legal title. |
| Sensitive cultural/ecological layers | Keep nonpublic by default and add only with the applicable Tribe, agency, or steward’s written authorization. | Authorized partner source only. |

> **Map rule:** A public overlay gives context; it does not establish ownership, access rights, work authorization, permitting, safety, cultural clearance, or legal boundaries.

## Professional Review Sequence

The practical order is to preserve the momentum of the workforce mission while avoiding premature clinical or legal promises. First, commission California nonprofit/healthcare counsel and a licensed clinical advisor to decide whether Project Reclaim remains a referral-and-workforce hub or enters a regulated-service model. Second, obtain insurance, employment, and safety review for field/shop work. Third, pursue qualified provider and public-agency MOUs. Fourth, activate only the data and map layers that have documented rights, privacy controls, and partner approval.

## Professional Review Checklist

This checklist is a pre-launch control for any future step beyond nonclinical workforce, training, material-recovery, and referral-navigation activity. Every item should be assigned to a named, qualified reviewer and recorded with its decision, date, responsible party, and any conditions before Project Reclaim presents the capability as live.

| Review domain | Required reviewer | Sign-off question |
|---|---|---|
| Entity, nonprofit, contracts, and real estate | California nonprofit/healthcare and real-estate counsel | Is the planned entity, site control, contract structure, procurement method, and public representation lawful and appropriate for the contemplated model? |
| Clinical model and co-occurring support | California-licensed clinical leader with the relevant behavioral-health/SUD expertise | Does the description accurately separate nonclinical work/training from licensed assessment, therapy, treatment, recovery planning, medication, and crisis response? |
| DHCS and local approvals | Regulatory counsel plus the applicable DHCS and county contacts | Does the work trigger DHCS licensing, certification, level-of-care designation, county requirements, or another approval path? [1] |
| Privacy, HIPAA, and Part 2 | Health-privacy counsel and a qualified privacy/security lead | What data, if any, is protected health information or Part 2 information; which consents, notices, business-associate arrangements, safeguards, and breach procedures apply? [4] [5] |
| Probation, parole, justice, and corrections coordination | Agency counsel/program lead plus Project Reclaim counsel | Is there a signed MOU or contract defining referrals, participation protections, reporting, data limits, liability, and termination? |
| Employment, wages, benefits, and worker classification | California labor/employment counsel and payroll/HR lead | Are jobs, internships, volunteer roles, apprenticeship, independent contractor, subcontract, and future enterprise arrangements classified and compensated lawfully? |
| Field, shop, vehicle, and tool safety | Safety professional, insurance broker/carrier, and operational lead | Are supervision, training, PPE, equipment, transportation, workers’ compensation, incident response, and site-safety controls sufficient for the work? |
| Housing and resident model | Housing counsel, licensed operator as applicable, and clinical partner | Does any residential model avoid coercive labor, maintain lawful tenancy/service arrangements, and clearly separate housing from treatment and employment? |
| Geospatial data and land overlays | GIS/data-rights reviewer, land-use counsel, and relevant landholder/agency | Do each source’s license, attribution, accuracy, access, privacy, tribal/cultural, and redistribution terms permit the intended map use? [6] [7] |
| Public claims and partner references | Executive lead and counsel | Are partner names, service claims, map layers, product availability, and participant stories verified, authorized, and free of implied endorsements? |

> **Release gate:** Do not publish an “active service,” a formal agency partnership, clinical availability, a residential program, or property-boundary overlay until the applicable review row has an affirmative written decision and the public copy matches that decision.
