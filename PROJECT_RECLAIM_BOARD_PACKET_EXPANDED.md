# PROJECT RECLAIM

## Board Packet Expansion: What We Are Asking to Build

**Prepared for:** Tribal, county, agency, philanthropic, and community partners
**Primary operating geography:** Mendocino and Lake Counties, California
**Program lead:** Tyler Morris
**Reference status:** Board discussion and planning document
**Public portal:** [https://reclaim.nexinus.net](https://reclaim.nexinus.net)

> **A note from Tyler **I am building Project Reclaim because I am tired of watching the same problems get passed from one system to another. People need a real path into useful work. Our forests and communities need real stewardship. Good wood should not automatically become waste, and people should not be written off because they are homeless, coming home from prison, veterans, dealing with recovery, or simply trying to get their life back on track.  My idea is straightforward: put people in a safe, structured environment, teach them skills that matter, give them a record of what they can actually do, and put that work toward places that need to be reclaimed. I am not presenting this as a finished government program, treatment center, or emergency agency. I am presenting a serious plan that I want to build with the right Tribal, county, fire, housing, workforce, business, and professional partners.I would rather show a board exactly what is still pending than make promises that cannot be backed up. That is why this packet separates the vision from the approvals, the property leads from secured sites, and the planning numbers from actual vendor bids.

> **Purpose of this packet **This document is the practical companion to the Project Reclaim Executive Launch Brief. It is designed to help a board or agency answer four questions: what is the first safe version of the program, what decisions are needed now, what evidence will prove progress, and what Project Reclaim is not yet authorized or equipped to claim.

## 1. The Decision in Front of the Board

I am asking for permission to develop a disciplined, partner-led pilot that connects four needs that are usually handled separately:

1. **Fire and land stewardship** — defensible-space work, vegetation management, access and preparedness, ecological restoration, and authorized disaster-readiness support.

1. **Wood and material recovery** — recovering usable wood, sorting it, documenting its source, and directing it toward reuse, education, building, craft, biomass, or community benefit.

1. **Workforce capability** — paid work, supervised training, credentials from approved issuers, work records, mentoring, and advancement.

1. **A stable path back into community life** — practical work and housing-readiness pathways supported by qualified partners, without pretending that a workforce program is a clinic, a jail, or an emergency service.

The immediate request is not approval to operate a treatment center, buy a property, place residents, deploy emergency crews, or represent a formal agency partnership. The immediate request is to authorize the **next diligence and mobilization stage** so the model can be tested lawfully and measured honestly.

### Requested board actions

- Authorize Project Reclaim to complete professional diligence on the two pilot-property leads and to return with a written go / hold / no-go recommendation.

- Approve partner conversations with county, Tribal, fire, housing, workforce, reentry, and local-business stakeholders using the role-specific asks in this packet.

- Identify one accountable board or sponsor representative for the first 90 days.

- Support a staged pilot budget that funds safety, site diligence, supervision, training, transportation, insurance, and evidence before expansion.

- Require a written release gate before any site, participant, partner, service, or impact claim is presented as active.

## 2. The First Version: Small Enough to Control, Real Enough to Prove

### Two-site hybrid model

**Site A — Stewardship Campus / residential-readiness site**

A rural or semi-rural property suitable for land stewardship, agriculture or vineyard-related work, future rehabilitation, and a carefully reviewed housing pathway. The campus is not treated as a residential program until habitability, fire and life safety, occupancy, staffing, insurance, service boundaries, and the legal structure are approved.

**Site B — Operations Hub / field-school and materials site**

A commercial or industrial property suitable for tool control, supervised field-school operations, storage, material sorting, hand-tool carpentry, and future wood processing. The operations hub must be reviewed for zoning, occupancy, access, fire protection, utilities, environmental hazards, insurance, and permitted use before production activity is represented as active.

### First cohort

The recommended first operating cohort is intentionally limited:

- 12 residential-readiness participants, only after the site and program controls are approved.

- 8 community-based participants in paid work or training pathways.

- 3 to 5 qualified supervisors or instructors, depending on the work scope.

- One accountable safety lead and one participant-support / referral coordinator.

- A separate relationship with qualified behavioral-health, recovery, housing, veterans, and reentry providers.

The first cohort is not a promise that every applicant will be accepted. Intake must include fit, safety, accessibility, transportation, work authorization, schedule, and support-boundary review. A person is not required to disclose a diagnosis or treatment history through the public portal.

### The pathway

**Discover → Train → Certify → Work → Contribute → Advance**

Participants should leave with more than attendance. Each participant’s work portfolio can document completed tasks, supervisor review, safety training, approved credentials, material or project contributions, and next-step recommendations. The portfolio is separate from clinical records, justice records, and private case management.

## 3. What the First 90 Days Actually Produce

The first 90 days should end with a reviewable evidence packet, not a large number of announcements.

### By day 30 — authority and safety

- Confirm the legal entity and contracting authority for the pilot activity.

- Obtain current broker or owner authorization for each property lead being evaluated.

- Open the property diligence register and assign every gate to a named reviewer.

- Adopt a basic safety manual, incident protocol, tool-control policy, vehicle policy, participant-rights notice, and grievance route.

- Confirm insurance and workers’ compensation requirements for the contemplated work.

- Identify qualified supervisors and instructors.

- Begin partner conversations with county planning/building, fire, workforce, housing, and community organizations.

- Buy or rent only low-risk mobilization equipment that has an approved use and responsible owner.

### By day 60 — controlled setup

- Complete initial property, site, zoning, access, utilities, fire, and occupancy findings.

- Establish the operating hub or temporary field setup only if the site authority and safety conditions permit it.

- Train the first participant group in orientation, rights, safety, communication, field conduct, and basic hand-tool work.

- Create the first curriculum modules and define the approved issuer for each credential.

- Establish the procurement register with quote, source, owner, condition, maintenance, insurance, and buy/rent/subcontract decision fields.

- Test the evidence workflow using a low-risk, authorized work package.

### By day 90 — evidence and decision

- Complete a defined work package with a documented scope, crew, dates, material pathway, and reviewer.

- Issue only credentials that are actually provided by an approved issuer; otherwise record the training as completed but not credentialed.

- Produce a participant work-portfolio summary with consent controls.

- Produce a property decision memo for each lead: proceed, hold, or reject, with the blockers named.

- Produce a partner status register showing who has responded, what role is proposed, what is authorized, and what remains pending.

- Present a board review packet with costs to date, safety incidents, lessons learned, verified outputs, participant feedback, and the recommended next stage.

## 4. Curriculum and Work Portfolio

The curriculum should be practical, modular, and tied to work that can be supervised safely.

| Module | Practical outcome | Possible credential source | Release rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orientation, rights, and workplace conduct | Understand expectations, grievance route, attendance, communication, and site rules | Project Reclaim completion record | May be issued internally as a completion record; not a state license |
| Basic field safety and first aid | Recognize hazards, use PPE, communicate, and respond within role limits | Approved safety / first-aid provider | Name the actual issuer and expiration date |
| Hand-tool stewardship | Use, inspect, maintain, and return hand tools | Project Reclaim supervised skills record | Record instructor and observed task |
| Fire-resilience fundamentals | Understand defensible space, fuels, access, and scope boundaries | Qualified fire or forestry training partner | Do not imply firefighter qualification |
| Vegetation and land stewardship | Perform approved low-risk vegetation, restoration, and grounds tasks | Qualified instructor or agency partner | Tie completion to a work record |
| Wood recovery and sorting | Grade, tag, store, and route recovered material | Project Reclaim / approved technical partner | Keep source and chain-of-custody fields clear |
| Carpentry foundations | Measure, layout, hand-tool joinery, repair, and safe shop practice | Approved trade or education partner | Do not call a participant an apprentice unless the program is formally registered or otherwise authorized |
| Equipment awareness | Understand machine zones, spotter communication, pre-use inspection, and lockout boundaries | Equipment vendor / qualified trainer | Awareness is not operator qualification |
| Workplace and life skills | Scheduling, conflict resolution, budgeting, documentation, and team accountability | Project Reclaim completion record or partner | Keep the language capability-focused, not therapeutic |
| Disaster-readiness support | Prepare supplies, clear authorized access, document work, and support partner-led readiness | Agency or qualified partner | Do not claim independent emergency authority |

### Credential and portfolio rules

- A credential is never invented by the portal.

- The issuer, course, date, expiration, and verification method must be recorded.

- A Project Reclaim work record can document observed capability without calling it a regulated license or outside certification.

- Participants can correct or withdraw public sharing of a portfolio item where the release agreement allows it.

- Public badges should say **“recorded,” “reviewed,” or “verified by [issuer]”** only when the underlying review exists.

## 5. Partner-Specific Asks

Project Reclaim should not approach every partner with the same generic request. Each conversation should have one clear purpose, one accountable contact, and one next decision.

### Tribal partners and Tribal EcoRestoration Alliance

**Ask:** Explore culturally grounded stewardship, community benefit, restoration priorities, workforce pathways, and appropriate boundaries for land, cultural knowledge, and data.

**What Project Reclaim brings:** A physical-work and materials-recovery platform, a local procurement model, a skills portfolio, and a commitment to keep cultural and sensitive geographic information private unless authorized.

**First meeting outcome:** Identify one potential stewardship scope and the review process required before any public reference is made.

### County planning, building, public works, and emergency partners

**Ask:** Clarify property, land-use, occupancy, access, vegetation, flood, infrastructure, and preparedness requirements; identify lawful scopes for community-support work.

**What Project Reclaim brings:** A defined pilot model and a willingness to document authorization, work orders, evidence, and release status.

**First meeting outcome:** Name the county reviewer and produce a written checklist for the selected pilot jurisdiction.

### CAL FIRE, forestry, and fire-resilience partners

**Ask:** Define training, supervision, and authorized non-emergency fire-resilience work that Project Reclaim can support without representing itself as a fire agency or hotshot crew.

**What Project Reclaim brings:** Participants, field supervision, basic tools, documentation, and local land-stewardship capacity.

**First meeting outcome:** Identify one approved training or prevention scope and its required qualifications.

### Housing and HUD-aligned partners

**Ask:** Explore housing stability, property rehabilitation, rural housing, community development, and referral alignment.

**What Project Reclaim brings:** A possible property-rehabilitation and workforce pathway, with housing and employment kept legally and operationally distinct.

**First meeting outcome:** Identify eligible programs and what Project Reclaim would need to become an eligible applicant or partner.

### Reentry, probation, parole, corrections, and justice partners

**Ask:** Explore lawful, voluntary referral pathways and practical work opportunities for people returning to the community.

**What Project Reclaim brings:** Supervised work, training, documented progress, and partner-enabled support referrals.

**First meeting outcome:** Determine whether a formal MOU is possible and what participant protections, data limits, and legal review are required.

### Local vendors, contractors, arborists, and equipment providers

**Ask:** Provide comparable quotes, service plans, used-equipment inspections, operator training, maintenance support, and subcontracting options.

**What Project Reclaim brings:** Local purchasing, recurring service demand if the pilot proves viable, and a clear specification instead of an informal shopping list.

**First meeting outcome:** Receive a quote or rental/subcontract alternative for one defined procurement package.

### Indigenous Wellness Alliance / Sage coordination

**Ask:** Discuss a future referral and information boundary, not a live integration claim.

**What Project Reclaim brings:** A nonclinical work and training pathway that may complement qualified wellness and reentry services.

**First meeting outcome:** Obtain the official product owner, approved endpoint and authentication documentation, minimum-data contract, consent language, and written agreement path before any technical connector is built.

## 6. Budget Envelopes for Board Discussion

The existing procurement package contains a **$4.34 million 100-person planning model**. That number is useful for long-range thinking but should not be presented as the amount required to start the first cohort. It excludes land, housing rehabilitation, payroll, ongoing operations, licensed services, project permits, financing, taxes, and several property-specific costs.

### Recommended staged envelopes

| Envelope | Purpose | Board use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mobilization envelope: $150,000–$250,000** | Diligence, insurance, safety design, basic PPE, hand tools, radios, transportation, professional review, and first training | Funds the work required before a site or cohort is represented as active |
| **Controlled pilot envelope: $450,000–$600,000** | Mobilization plus limited supervision, participant supports, site setup, rentals, initial work packages, evidence, and operating reserve | Appropriate ask for a first 90-day / first-stage pilot, subject to property and partner decisions |
| **Two-site establishment envelope: **$$1.2M–$$**2.0M** | Site control, rehabilitation, staffing, transportation, equipment, insurance, and early operations | Used only after the property and operating model pass diligence |
| **100-person scale planning model: approximately $4.34M capital** | Fleet, field equipment, safety, shop, storage, and launch-readiness allowances | Strategic capital plan; not a bid and not a full residential operating budget |

### What must be quoted before purchase

- Vehicles, trailers, loaders, chippers, sawmills, shop equipment, and major safety systems.

- Insurance, workers’ compensation, auto, property, liability, and any participant or residential coverage.

- Property lease, purchase, utilities, rehabilitation, permits, inspections, and professional services.

- Payroll, benefits, taxes, supervision, transportation, food, participant support, and operating reserve.

- Any clinical, recovery, housing, or justice-partner service delivered by an outside provider.

### Procurement control

Every purchase should pass a buy / rent / lease / subcontract decision. The decision should identify the work need, authorized site, qualified operator, safety plan, maintenance path, insurance requirement, comparable quote, and person responsible for the asset.

## 7. Evidence Dashboard: What the Board Should See

The portal should report only information that has a source, date, owner, and review state.

### Initial measures

**People**

- Number of participants who entered an approved pathway.

- Number who completed orientation and safety training.

- Number of work records reviewed by a supervisor.

- Number of credentials issued by an approved issuer.

- Number of participants who advanced, exited, or requested a correction.

**Land and fire resilience**

- Authorized work packages opened and closed.

- Acres or linear feet only when measured and reviewed.

- Vegetation or access tasks completed only when evidence supports the claim.

- Before/after documentation only when consent, property authority, and release approval exist.

**Materials**

- Material received, sorted, processed, stored, reused, sold, donated, or directed to biomass.

- Material source and chain-of-custody record.

- Reuse project or community destination.

**Local economy**

- Local vendors contacted, quoted, selected, and paid.

- Contractor and equipment-provider partnerships verified.

- Maintenance and service commitments.

**Safety and governance**

- Training and qualification status.

- Incidents, near misses, corrective actions, and unresolved risks.

- Open diligence gates and overdue reviews.

- Public records released, retired, or corrected.

### Public status language

Use **Planning**, **Proposed**, **Authorization Pending**, **Active**, **Evidence Pending**, **Verified**, **Completed**, **Closed**, or **Unverified**. Avoid using a photo, map pin, form, or future roadmap item as proof that work is occurring.

## 8. Risk Register and Controls

| Risk | What could go wrong | First control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property suitability | A promising listing is not available, permitted, habitable, or safe | Treat every lead as unverified until broker, title, planning, code, utilities, fire, and professional reviews are complete |
| Participant safety | A person is placed into work without adequate supervision, PPE, training, or support | Start with low-risk tasks, named supervisors, documented orientation, and a stop-work rule |
| Coercion or exploitation | Housing, justice referral, or program participation is confused with forced labor | Separate housing, employment, services, and referral agreements; provide rights, grievance, and exit processes |
| Clinical overreach | The portal or staff imply therapy, treatment, crisis care, or medical services | Use qualified external providers and publish a clear nonclinical boundary |
| Data exposure | Health, SUD, justice, property, or cultural information is placed in a general system | Minimize collection, role-restrict access, and keep sensitive records outside the portal unless a compliant structure exists |
| Public-land or map misuse | A map overlay is mistaken for ownership, access, or permission | Record source, license, date, precision, and authorization; label maps as context |
| Equipment liability | Equipment is purchased before there is a qualified operator or approved work order | Buy only after utilization, training, maintenance, insurance, and authorization gates pass |
| Partner misrepresentation | A conversation is presented as an endorsement or formal partnership | Use a partner registry with status, source, named contact, and written authorization |
| Overexpansion | The organization tries to launch residential, clinical, emergency, industrial, and digital systems at once | Sequence the work: authority, safety, one pilot, evidence, review, then scale |
| Evidence failure | Public impact claims cannot be proven later | Require source, date, reviewer, release decision, and correction path for every public metric |

## 9. The Board Review Packet at Day 90

The board should receive one folder containing:

1. A one-page decision memo.

1. Property diligence scorecards and unresolved blockers.

1. Current insurance and safety status.

1. Participant-rights and intake summary, without protected case details.

1. Training modules, issuers, and completed work records.

1. Procurement register with quotes and buy/rent/subcontract decisions.

1. Authorized work package with scope, crew, evidence, and reviewer.

1. Financial report showing committed, spent, and remaining funds.

1. Partner register with responses and next actions.

1. Safety incidents, near misses, corrections, and lessons learned.

1. Public portal release log showing what is visible and why.

1. A recommendation to proceed, pause, redesign, or stop.

The board should not be asked to approve expansion because the website looks complete. It should be asked to approve expansion because the evidence shows that the model is safe, lawful, useful, and financially understandable.

## 10. Final Position

I believe Project Reclaim can become a powerful regional platform because it starts with work people can see: maintaining land, reducing risk, recovering materials, repairing properties, creating useful products, and building a record of capability.

The credibility of the model will come from restraint as much as ambition. A board, Tribe, county, agency, or funder should be able to see exactly what is real, what is proposed, what is authorized, what is pending, and what Project Reclaim needs from them next.

The first win is not a 100-person compound. The first win is one safe, authorized, well-supervised work package that improves a real place, gives people a real record of contribution, and leaves behind evidence that another partner can trust.

> **I am ready to move Project Reclaim from vision to disciplined proof.**
